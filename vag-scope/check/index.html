Don’t trust the report. Check the car.

Someone shared a VAG Scope report with you. A report can be edited, or come from another car. Run the same mileage and VIN check yourself, on the car in front of you, with your phone and a Bluetooth adapter.

Three steps

1. Get an adapter.

A Bluetooth ELM327 adapter that plugs into the car’s OBD socket, usually under the dashboard on the driver’s side.

2. Install VAG Scope.

On an Android phone. No adapter yet? Open a demo car first to see what the checks show.

3. Run the checks.

Ignition on, connect, then run the mileage check and the VIN check. Each one shows its evidence and gives you your own PDF report.

What the checks tell you

Mileage.

VAG Scope reads the distance stored in every control unit that keeps one, compares it with the dashboard, and shows its working: which units agree, which do not, and the margin it allowed.

VIN.

It compares the VIN on the windscreen, the data sticker and the body with the VIN stored in every control unit, and names any unit that comes from another car.

What they can’t tell you.

A result is a clue, never a guarantee: units can be swapped or changed. Read it next to the service history, and have the car inspected.

Works on Volkswagen, Audi, Seat and Škoda cars using TP 2.0 (about 2004–2010) or UDS (about 2012 and later). Not on cars with 29-bit diagnostic ids only, such as the ID. electric models and the Golf 8 generation.
